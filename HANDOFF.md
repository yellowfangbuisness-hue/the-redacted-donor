# The Redacted Donor

Open **The Redacted Donor.app** on an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 13 or later. Choose **Begin investigation** to take Mateo’s seat across from the recruiter. Progress saves on the device. **Continue investigation** restores the case, evidence, purchases and position. After the rescue the same button opens the completed dossier.

The project also includes the entire offline game in `index.html`. Open it directly in a WebGL 2-capable desktop browser. The app and each browser maintain their own saves. The game loads its geometry, title illustration, shaders and synthesized audio locally.

## Playing

| Input | Action |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D | Walk |
| Mouse, click-drag or arrow keys | Look |
| E or click while facing a nearby object | Interact |
| Hold E | Pound the window or peel its film |
| C | Toggle crouch |
| Shift | Run |
| B or Tab | Open the lab coat kit |
| J | Read recovered clues and action cards |
| Esc or P | Pause |
| Drag or use arrows during inspection | Rotate the exhibit |
| F during inspection | Turn the exhibit over |

The touch controls provide a movement stick, drag-to-look, a crouch button and an interaction button. The native View menu supports full screen.

## Case sequence

- In **The Blind Street**, pound on the window, then take the pocket watch from the west bench. The watch adds the clinic clock to the HUD.
- Each file opens with the detective examining a document under the desk lamp. Turning it over starts Mateo’s memory. A briefing card lists the case question, the rules and all six leads, each marked ☀ day, ☾ night or ◐ any time.
- **The Golden Ticket:** read the ad, collect carbon paper, align the carbon copy (drag puzzle), read the ledger at night, emboss the receipt, hide it under the radiator. The detective then opens the drawer with 409, checks the recruitment archive and pins the lead.
- **The Cold Room:** examine the window prints and cooler stencil at night, take the seal impression, find the key glint, match the seal to a gate ticket (ticket puzzle), hide the key. The detective retrieves the key, opens the closet, checks the freight tag and pins the lead.
- **The Redacted Donor:** read the printer ribbon and waiver at night, copy the radio frequency, find the advocate’s card, tune the receiver to 640 kHz (dial puzzle), leave the card in the printer. The detective checks the call log, pins the lead, radios the team and strips the window tint.

## Cutscenes, searching and navigation

Major beats play a short cutscene. Scene changes (entering or leaving a memory, taking the watch, closing a case) use a negative-energy transition: the image inverts with burning white outlines, the frame ripples, and ink tendrils close in before receding on the new scene. The guard's stare bleeds the same ink in from the edges. The camera eases to a framed shot, letterbox bars slide in with a caption, and a screen effect matches the moment: a flash and evidence stamp, a sepia memory ripple, an iris close, radio static or dawn light. The beats are the intake door locking, taking the watch, entering a memory, solving each puzzle, hiding evidence, opening the drawer and closet, recovering the key, corroborating a lead, closing a case file, the radio call and the dawn ending. Press E, Space or Esc to skip.

A records annex opens through a doorway in the south partition. It holds patient-file shelving, a waiting bench, a coat rack, a typist’s desk, a safe, an examination gurney behind a screen, and a medicine cabinet. Twelve props across the clinic can be searched, including a wanted poster beside the street window (`src/art/wanted.jpg`), for +25 points each. Each one reveals a short document about the clinic. The journal counts them, and the map marks unsearched ones with a dot.

An arrow on the screen edge points toward the current lead whenever it is off screen, with the distance beneath. The recruiter and guard are sculpted 3D figures, and props use rounded, lit geometry.

## Three rooms and the gates between them

The clinic is three rooms joined by doors that open as the story advances.
- **Room 1, the intake lobby** (the original office) holds Case 1. After Case 1 is pinned, the lab door stays shut until the detective cuts the recruitment terminal's lure server (wire it to the moderators, then snip three cables) and sprays the door keypad with **luminol** (150 points) to read the latent code 4 0 9.
- **Room 2, the phlebotomy lab** holds Case 2: the transit chest with seal K7, the lab radiator hiding the closet key, the padlocked supply closet (code 0 4 0, the gate ticket's departure time), a centrifuge bench and a blood-draw chair. The **UV flashbulb** (200 points) is needed to read the painted-over stencil and the handprints. After Case 2, the detective shreds the surgeon's transplant-tourism licence and stamps an Istanbul Declaration ethics hold on Mateo's file, which opens the dispatch door.
- **Room 3, the dispatch office** holds Case 3: the teleprinter ribbon, the bleached waiver (UV), the duty roster and the advocate's card. The climax is breaking the emergency glass for the dispatch key, tuning the vacuum-tube ham radio's frequency and squelch dials until the oscilloscope trace is clean, and peeling the window tint at dawn by dragging it down in strips.

Every interaction is hands-on: click an object, then drag, pull, turn, spray or smash in its own view. Guards patrol the lab and dispatch office with a floor sight cone (4 m by day, 3.5 m at night, 20% less with crepe soles; 45° to each side). In the standard game they also hear footsteps (walking carries 1.7 m, running 4.5 m, crouching or standing still is silent, crepe soles halve it) and come to look; sense a standing player within 1.1 m whichever way they face; chase at about the player's walking speed and run on to where they last saw them before searching for 7 seconds; stay wary for 25 seconds after a sighting (20% longer sight); and call other guards within 10 m. Veteran mode keeps the gentler guards (no hearing, no close sense, shorter search). Constants are at the top of `stealth.js`. When a guard spots the player, the spot sting plays, then the chase music (`tension`) starts at once, loud, and runs until 7 seconds after he last saw them (`tenseT` in `game.js`). The heartbeat follows `pulse` (0 to 1, how much danger the player is in): one beat every 1.05 s and soft when a guard is merely near, down to one every 0.37 s and loud while he watches or chases. In the detective's sections two jazz tracks play in turn (`audio.jazz`); they stop for Mateo's memories and during a chase. Walking bobs the view once per footstep with a slight roll (`bobAmount` in `move()`; gentler in Veteran mode, off under reduced motion). Crouch behind furniture, or pick up something to throw, hold the mouse to aim and release to throw it; the crash pulls a guard away for six seconds. Throwables (`KINDS` in `stealth.js`, names and landing sounds in `THROWN` in `game.js`): beakers and medicine bottles shatter, a glass ashtray clinks, paperweights and enamel mugs clank, ledgers thud. Thirteen are placed, including three in the intake office (desk ashtray, ledger on the rest bench, bottle on the tall cabinet). The watch overdrive (250 points, O key) jams the guards for 8 seconds, then recharges for 45 (`JAM_COOLDOWN`); it works in Mateo's memories, where it also flips day and night, and in the detective's guarded stages. A recorded day/night sting (`src/audio/daynight.mp3`) plays 2.5 seconds before every shift change.

**Veteran Investigator Mode** (title screen, pause menu or the HUD button under the points) leaves the default game untouched and adds: about 1.5× reading size with no italics, a 2× loupe over documents, large buttons in place of every drag, click-and-hold instead of rapid clicking, 20% brighter nights, a brass pulse on interactive objects, a 30% slower patrol and a 2.5 s yellow warning with a heartbeat before a guard's stare starts to count. Any open document, journal or inspection already pauses the clock.

## Day–night and the guard

One second of play advances the clinic clock fifteen minutes. Each day or night shift lasts 48 seconds. The HUD dial shows the sun or moon and the time until the next shift. Night turns the room blue and moonlit. Day brings warm light through the blinds. Sitting on the staff bench skips straight to the next shift. Night leads collected in the first eight seconds of a night earn a +50 bonus.

The guard telegraphs in stages. Footsteps and keys play first, with an eye banner reading “Footsteps approaching”. Next, an amber search cone sweeps the floor from the east door. If he sees you, the banner turns red and shakes, the screen closes in with a red vignette and scan lines, glare comes from the door, and a meter fills. Crouch (C) or break line of sight before it fills, or you lose a composure bulb. Losing all three ends the file: −50 points, retry from the desk.

## Development

The shipped app and single-file game are ready to run. To rebuild them from source, install the versions of Three.js and esbuild in `package.json` (`npm install`; `node_modules/` is ignored by git), then run `npm run build`. `npm install` also fetches `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` (pinned), which the build packs into `hand-tracking.js`. The browser tests also need `playwright-core` (`npm install --no-save playwright-core`, run it after any `npm install`, which removes it) and run with `INK_TOOLCHAIN="$PWD"`. `npm run build:mac` also compiles the AppKit host and packages the Apple silicon app. It requires the macOS Command Line Tools. `INK_TOOLCHAIN` can point to a separate directory containing those Node dependencies. The build keeps `mars-rehearsal.html` in sync with the main game for existing local links.

`src/campaign.js` owns scoring, clue prerequisites, checkpoints and purchases. `src/game.js` runs the first-person controls, interactions and HUD. `src/room.js` and `src/props.js` build the office and animate its occupants. `src/renderer.js` applies the split-toned noir grade, day–night lighting and dawn pass. `src/shifts.js` owns the clock and guard patrol. `src/fieldwork.js` holds the three hands-on puzzles. `src/floorplan.js` holds collision footprints and draws the journal map. `src/annex.js` builds the lab and dispatch-office furniture. `src/tactile.js` holds the hands-on interaction views. `src/stealth.js` runs the roaming guards and throwables. `src/figures.js` builds the 3D characters. `native/Game.swift` handles the desktop window, relative mouse and application lifecycle. `src/casebook.js` contains the authored case files, dialogue evidence and advocacy cards.

## Online records (Supabase)
- Project `redacted-donor` in the **Oak Hackathon database** org (ref `raemznzmcfzqwtuskwvq`, ap-south-1); schema in `supabase/schema.sql`. URL and publishable key are baked in by `build.mjs` (override with `SUPABASE_URL` / `SUPABASE_KEY`); the CSP `connect-src` allows only that URL.
- No sign-in: each device makes a random secret key (`redacted-donor.cloud.key` in localStorage). The database stores only its SHA-256 hash.
- Tables (`players`, `scores`, `runs`, `saves`) have no direct write access. Writes go through functions that hash the key and act only on that player: `set_name`, `submit_score` (plausibility checks, 20 s throttle, returns board place), `log_run`, `push_save` / `pull_save`, `load_save_by_code`. Names and scores are public; key hashes, runs and saves are not.
- `src/cloud.js`: small fetch client with a local outbox, so offline results upload later. The game never waits on the network.
- **Accounts** (title menu → *Sign in* / *Account · NAME*): email + password, or investigator ID + password (stored as `<id>@players.redacted-donor.app`), or keep playing as a guest. "Confirm email" is **off** in Supabase Auth because the built-in mailer only reaches team members. Signed in, `player_for()` uses `players.user_id`, so progress, cloud save and best scores follow the account; a new account takes over the device's guest progress. Signing out resets the device to a fresh guest. `my_stats()` feeds the account page.
- Signing in on a device that already has a save opens **Your progress**: transfer that save to the account (replacing the account's) or delete it (use the account's save, or start fresh). A deleted save is kept from coming back by a local "ignore cloud saves before" timestamp. Every player row has a name: the database defaults it to `Agent NNNN`; new accounts use the investigator ID, the guest's name, or the start of the email.
- `players.email` mirrors the account's sign-in email (filled by `player_for()`); it is not readable through the API, only in the dashboard. Investigator-ID accounts show `<id>@players.redacted-donor.app`; guests are blank.
- **Privacy policy / Terms of service**: text in `src/legal.js`, opened from the Account page. The Privacy page has **Delete my account and online data**, which calls `delete_me()` (removes the player row, its scores, runs and save, and the auth user).
- UI: title **Records** (standard/veteran leaderboards + load a 12-char transfer code), ending **Submit to the record**, pause **Cloud save on/off** with the transfer code.

## Normal maps, performance and the Windows version (2026-11-15)
- **Normal maps on walls and floor:** `InkTextureFactory.relief()` (`room.js`) works a normal map out of each painted texture (darker paint is lower; half the texture's size). The walls and floor stay unlit, so their colours are unchanged; `sheetMaterial()` adds a few shader lines that brighten or darken each pixel by how far its normal leans toward the window light (`reliefLight`). A flat pixel is multiplied by exactly 1, so only board gaps, mouldings, the wallpaper pattern and grain gain light and shadow. Strength is the `1.15` in that shader; `depth` in `relief()` sets how steep the normals are.
- **Measured cost** (this Mac, 1280×800, frame cap off): 2.7 to 4.4 ms a frame, 30 to 264 draw calls, up to 217,000 triangles; the lab is the heaviest room. `window.__info()` (with `?spatialtest`) reports draw calls, triangles and the current resolution scale.
- **Adaptive resolution:** `keepPace()` in `game.js` watches the average frame time. Slower than 26 ms for two seconds draws the picture a step smaller (down to 60%); faster than 12.5 ms steps back up. Nothing changes on a machine that keeps up. It is paused while hand tracking is on, because the camera model's cost is not a drawing cost.
- **Sounds:** recorded sounds are unpacked one at a time, smallest first, instead of all at once when you press Begin (`warmRecorded()` in `sound.js`).
- **Windows version:** `npm run build:win` (or `sh native/windows.sh` after a build) makes the folder `The Redacted Donor (Windows)` and a zip of it: the same `index.html`, `Play The Redacted Donor.cmd` (opens the game in its own Edge or Chrome window, or the default browser) and `README.txt`. Sources are in `native/windows/` (CRLF line endings). There is no `.exe`: no Windows compiler is on this Mac, and every Windows 10/11 PC has Edge. Checked: the game loads, saves and starts hand tracking from a `file://` address in Chrome on the Mac. Not checked: the launcher on a real Windows PC.

## Animated cut-out characters
The recruiter, the enforcer, the guards and the orderly are the same painted cut-outs as before, now animated (`src/sprites.js`). Nothing was redrawn: each character's original painter takes a `pose` and bends its own drawing, like a paper puppet. For the recruiter, enforcer and guards (`portrait()` in `props.js`) the two halves of the coat below the split lift in turn like legs, the whole figure bounces and rocks from the feet, and the head and hat tilt on the neck; the orderly (`annex.js`) rocks and bobs as one piece.
- `makeSheet(key, { w, h, paint })` paints a sprite sheet once: 20 cells (idle 2, walk 6, run 6, interact 4, look 2), poses from `poseOf()`.
- `SpriteSheetAnimator` shows one cell by moving the texture's UV offset, 12 drawings a second whatever the frame rate, on a plane that turns about the vertical axis only to face the camera. The art faces screen-right and is mirrored when the character heads to the camera's left.
- `Actor` adds a body: `goTo([[x, z], …])` walks waypoints with the floor plan's collision (`blocked()`, the same footprints the player uses); each axis stops on its own when something is in the way, so a character slides along a desk rather than passing through it.
- `Cutscene` runs steps in order: `walk`, `wait`, `act`, `turn`, `call`, `together`.
- **Opening scene** (`animate()` in `props.js`, on real time because the game clock stands still under the contract panel): the recruiter paces behind the desk; on the second beat he comes to the middle, leans in and slides the affidavit across; if Mateo refuses, the enforcer comes in by the staff door, round the end of the desk, and reaches across; if he signs, Mateo's hand signs and snaps the nib (`makeHandSheet`); then both men walk out round the desk to the door.
- **Guards** (`stealth.js`, sheet passed in as `guardSheet`): walk on patrol, run in a chase, stop with the look pose when searching.
- **Finale**: as the tint is peeled (`strip`), the street walkers stop one after another, turn to the window and look in. They keep their original figures and swinging legs.
- Footstep noise for the guards ignores jumps of more than half a metre in a frame, so loading a save or ending a cutscene is not heard as running.

## Voice lines
The 54 recorded lines live in `src/audio/voice/<id>.mp3` (ids and text in `VOICELINES.md`); `build.mjs` embeds them as `vo/<id>`. They were converted from the team's `.m4a` takes with silence trimmed and loudness evened out; the radio lines (`dispatch-01`, `dispatch-02`, `advocate-01`) have a band-pass radio effect baked in. `audio.say(...ids)` in `sound.js` plays lines one after another, one voice at a time. The opening scene calls it directly (`contract()` in `game.js`: Mateo's answer, then the recruiter and the narrator). Every other line is matched by text: `speak()` checks whatever a caption (`notify`), a cutscene line or a puzzle remark shows against the `VOICE` phrase table, so the voice says what the subtitle shows. To add or re-record a line, drop the file in `src/audio/voice/` and, if it is new, add its phrase to `VOICE`. Not recorded: `narr-02`, `mateo-17` (the hurt gasp) and the optional object lines.

## Controller
Any connected gamepad works (standard layout), handled by `drivePad()` in `game.js` every frame. Roaming: left stick walks, right stick looks (or turns the document under the lamp), LT or a left-stick click runs, and the buttons send the same key events as the keyboard (`PAD_KEYS`: A = E, B = C, X = J, Y = B, LB = F, RT/RB = G, Back = O, Start = Esc). On the title screen, in a menu, a cutscene or a puzzle, the sticks and D-pad move the on-screen pointer instead, A presses, grabs and drags with it and B or Start goes back; this goes through `pointerInput()`, the same path a tracked hand uses. The pointer only appears once the controller has been touched. The Controls page is one table: each action on a row with its key and its controller button side by side. Typing (sign-in, leaderboard name) still needs a keyboard.

## Spatial hand tracking (optional webcam mode)
**Off by default. With it off (or no camera) nothing changes:** `handNav` in `game.js` is zero, so `move()` computes exactly what it did, and none of the hand code runs. With it on, the hands grab things and turn the view; **walking is keyboard-only** (WASD), and mouse and keyboard still work alongside the hands.

### Controls (hands grab and look; the keyboard walks)
| gesture | does |
|---|---|
| Hand moves | Pointer (amber ring) and the player's own 3D hand, drawn where they hold it. Past the comfort zone (70% of the reach from the resting point) the view turns, faster the further out (up to 1.3 rad/s) |
| Grab: close the hand (about half closed is enough) or pinch | Takes hold of what is under the hand; opening the hand lets go. Nothing moves by being touched with an open hand. Over a button: click; grab-and-drag scrolls a long page. In the room: interact (same as E; a held grab = held E). Holding a throwable: hold to aim, release to throw. In a cutscene: skip |
| Both hands grabbing | Turn/tilt the document under the lamp; rotate/scale the notebook page |
| Fist pushed at the camera | Bang on the street window; strike the glass case |
| Keyboard | Walk, run and crouch, as without hand tracking |

**The drawn hands** (`limb()` in `hands.js`): white cartoon gloves (fat rounded fingers, a puffy pad over the back of the hand, a rolled cuff), built from 21 joints and 23 bones per hand and posed every frame from the tracked landmarks, so they move as the real hand does. The mirrored picture gives left/right and up/down; the world landmarks give the fingers' curl toward the surface, as if looking at the back of your own hand. Over a puzzle the hand hovers 7.5 cm above the board; in the room it floats 42 cm in front of the camera. The pointer (the point that grabs) sits among the fingers, ahead of the palm, so the thing being grabbed is not hidden. Hands are hidden over menus, cutscenes and setup.
**Steadiness:** the pointer has its own heavier filter (`OneEuro(.9, 3.5)`), and `cleanProfile` widens any calibrated reach narrower than 44% × 34% of the camera picture, so a small hand movement cannot cross the screen. A press on a button counts on release even if the hand drifted off it while closing.
**Grab assist:** when a hand closes just beside something in a puzzle, it takes the nearest grabbable thing within 6% of the screen height (`onDown` in `hands.js`); the mouse stays exact.
**Locks:** no turning while grabbing, while a puzzle, document, menu, cutscene, setup or tutorial is up, or while the pointer is over a button. After any of those, the hand must come back to rest once before it steers again (`lookReady`).
Not reachable by hand: walking, typing (sign-in, leaderboard name, padlock digits have buttons), and the watch-overdrive key (O).

### Looking down, and changing hand tracking mid-game (2026-11-15)
- **Looking down** no longer needs the hand near the bottom of the camera's picture. A short dip below the resting point starts it, and it is at full speed while the whole hand is still in view (`LOOK_FLOOR`, `DOWN_DEAD`, `DOWN_FULL` in `spatial.js`): the dip is measured against a reach that ends no lower than 80% of the way down the frame, however far down the player reached during setup. Up, left and right are unchanged.
- **H** turns hand tracking on or off anywhere: the title screen, a menu, an open puzzle, a cutscene or the room (not while typing in a text box). Inside Pause the menu redraws to show the change. **Pause** has "Hand tracking: on/off", "Hand look speed: slow / normal / fast" (saved as `redacted-donor.hands.speed`; 0.6×, 1×, 1.5×), "Recalibrate hands" and "Hand tutorial". Turning it off shows a short note that mouse and keyboard play as before.
- Checks: the look test in `tools/spatial.test.mjs`, and section 8 of `tools/spatial-browser.test.mjs`.

### Tangled hands, blur and the two-hand turn (2026-11-15)
- **Clapping, laced fingers, one hand crossing the other:** the camera model cannot read touching hands (landmarks jump, left and right swap, one hand vanishes into the other). `Gestures.feed` now spots this (`TOUCH`, `NEAR` in `spatial.js`: two palm centres within one palm length, or one hand vanishing while within 1.7 palm lengths of the other and closing on it at 3 palm lengths a second or more) and holds everything: the pointer stays where it was, a held grab lets go once, and no grab, knock or two-hand turn fires. The frame carries `tangled: true` and the status line says "Hands are touching: move them apart". After the hands have been apart for `SETTLE` (0.18 s), or a single hand has stayed for `MERGED` (0.45 s), the hands are told apart afresh. A second hand that merely drops out of view beside the first is not treated as a merge.
- **Blur:** no grab, pinch, fist or knock starts on a hand moving faster than `FAST` (2.4 picture heights a second). One already held is kept.
- **Two-hand turn:** ignored while the hands are within 1.6 palm lengths, and a jump of more than 0.6 rad or 60% in one frame is treated as a misread.
- MediaPipe's presence and tracking confidence are raised to 0.6.
- Tests: three new cases in `tools/spatial.test.mjs` (a scripted clap, a grab let go on touch, a blurred hand).

### Camera choice, speed and the tracking budget (2026-11-15)
- **Another camera** (an iPhone through Continuity Camera, a USB webcam): "Switch camera" beside the title-screen toggle, or "Camera: name" in Pause, steps through the cameras the system offers (`spatial.cameras()`, `useCamera()`); the choice is saved as `redacted-donor.hands.camera`. The Mac app's `Info.plist` has `NSCameraUseContinuityCameraDeviceType` so an iPhone can appear there. Not tested with a real iPhone.
- **Two engines** read the camera (`createSpatial` in `spatial.js`). *Background* (the default): the model runs in a worker, so the game never waits for it; the camera is asked for 60 pictures a second. *Main*: the model on the game's own thread at about 30 a second, as it was at first. The game uses the background engine whenever it starts; if it reads slower than 32 ms a picture (it could not reach the graphics card), it tries the main thread on the same pictures for a second and keeps whichever is clearly quicker (`compare()`, `settle()`). Pause has "Hand engine: smooth / classic" (`redacted-donor.hands.engine`).
- **Packed files:** `build.mjs` writes `hand-tracking.js` beside `index.html`: the MediaPipe code, its WebAssembly and the hand model (`src/vendor/hand_landmarker.task`) as one script. `loadAssets()` loads it with a script tag (this works from a file as well as a server), so tracking starts in under a second and needs no internet. If the file is missing the game downloads the same files (`VISION`, `MODEL`). Each thread makes its own blob addresses from the bytes (`addresses()`), because a page opened from a file cannot hand its blob addresses to a worker. The page policy allows `'self' file: blob:` for this.
- **Glide** (`Glide` in `spatial.js`): between camera pictures the pointer carries on at the speed it was last seen moving, for at most 45 ms, and each new picture is blended in. On a 120 or 240 Hz display the pointer then moves on every drawn frame instead of stepping at the camera's rate. A hand that is slowing is taken to go on slowing, so it eases into a stop; after a dead stop from full speed it can run on by at most one picture of travel before it is drawn back.
- **Lead:** the pointer runs `LEAD` (0.04 s) ahead of the steadied hand, but never by more than twice what the hand is seen to be ahead of it, so it cannot overshoot a hand that has stopped.
- **Measured** (this Mac, headless Chrome, a real photo of two hands fed in as the camera): both engines find both hands at 30 pictures a second, about 25 ms a picture; with the background engine the page keeps 60 frames a second, with the main one it drops to about 50. The status line under the hand panel shows pictures a second, milliseconds a picture and "background" (`spatial.stats`).
- **Camera care:** during setup the panel shows the camera's own picture behind the wireframe; "Too dark to see your hands: add light" appears when the picture's average brightness is very low (`light()`); a camera that stalls gives no hands after 0.7 s instead of a frozen one; a camera that is unplugged turns tracking off with a note (`spatial.onlost`).
- Privacy policy and terms (`legal.js`) cover the camera, the camera choice and what is remembered.

### Gestures added from the final brief (`~/Downloads/message (4).txt`)
- **Wrist turn:** a closed hand turning at the wrist reports `roll` (radians, clockwise as the player sees it); `hands.turn()` passes it to whatever is held. The lock wheels (one digit per 0.4 rad), the radio dials and the watch respond.
- **Open-palm strike:** a flat palm driven at the camera at twice a fist push's speed and depth also counts as a knock (`strike` in `Track.update`).
- **Flick:** `flick` on release is the hand's speed; a throw uses the larger of the hold time and the flick.
- **Panel:** `#spatialView` is a blurred glass panel with a white wireframe; thumb and index tips glow amber while a grab is registered (`drawHands`).
- **Lean:** walking the rooms, the picture is drawn from an eye shifted up to 1.6 cm with the hand (`lean` in `frame()`); off in puzzles, menus, cutscenes, Veteran mode and reduced motion.
- **One hand reported twice** by the model is treated as one hand, not as two hands touching.
- **A second, curled hand** no longer blocks grabs: the two-hand turn only forms where something can be turned (`spatial.twoHands`).
- **Grabbing and letting go:** closing or opening the fingers slides the palm a little. If the hand was at rest just before (`rest()` in `Gestures`), the grab or release lands where it was holding still (`f.at`), and the pointer stays shifted by that much while it holds. A release on the move lands where the hand is.
- **Dials and lock wheels by hand:** a held dial turns by sliding the hand sideways or by turning the wrist, whichever the hand starts doing, never both, and never by circling round the knob (`onMove` and `turn` in `hands.js`; `slide: true` on the radio dials and the watch). Lock wheels step by dragging up or down, or by the wrist.
- **Which hand points after a mix-up:** when the model gives both hands the same label, the pointing hand is the one on the side of the picture it has been seen working on (`side` in `Gestures`), not the side its label names (the model names hands as if the picture were mirrored).
- **A holding hand** may vanish for up to 0.45 s (a blur mid-drag) before it lets go; an idle one is dropped after 0.25 s.
- **Desk clutter puzzle:** thin pieces (glasses, keys) have an unseen plate behind them so the whole piece can be grabbed.
- Not done: a pointer driven by the fingertips (it jumps on every pinch), half-resolution passes or WebGPU.

### Files
- `src/spatial.js`
  - `OneEuro`: adaptive filter. Used on all 21 picture landmarks and 21 world landmarks per hand and on the pointer. The speed term is scaled by hand size so near and far hands filter alike.
  - `Gestures.feed(result, t)`: takes MediaPipe's `{landmarks, worldLandmarks, handedness}`. Every threshold is a ratio of palm length (wrist 0 → middle knuckle 9). With world landmarks, finger ratios are measured in 3D and depth comes from a least-squares fit of picture against world; without them it falls back to the 2D picture. Pinch, grip, fist and push each have separate engage/release thresholds plus a hold time (`Latch`), and a hand may vanish for 0.25 s (`LOST`) without dropping anything. A **grab** is a pinch or a grip (the four fingertips' average distance from the wrist below `gripOn`); the frame's `down` / `up` are its edges. Each hand in `frame.hands` carries what it is drawn from: `lm` (mirrored picture landmarks), `w` (world landmarks) and `screen` (where it is on screen).
  - Hand roles (`point` / `other`) are locked per tracked hand. Each camera frame, detections are paired with the hands already followed by cost: same label = distance, other label = distance + 0.3 (so a flickering label on a still hand is forgiven), and labels alone decide when a new hand has just come in. A hand that jumps or is lost and found keeps its role and its grab; a hand mislabelled on entry is corrected after 0.6 s idle. The two-hand angle is measured from pointing hand to other hand, so crossing hands does not flip it.
  - `Calibration`: the four setup steps as a pure state machine. `cleanProfile` / `loadProfile` / `saveProfile`: the profile in `localStorage['redacted-donor.hands.v1']` (`pointer`, `neutral`, `palm`, `pinchOn/Off`, `fistOn/Off`, `gripOn/Off`, `pushOn/Off`, `pushDepth`, `bounds`; a profile saved before `gripOn/Off` existed gets the standard values).
  - `createSpatial()`: camera + MediaPipe hand landmarker (tasks-vision 0.10.14 from jsDelivr, model from storage.googleapis.com, fetched only when turned on).
- `src/game.js` ("Spatial hand tracking" and "Hand setup" blocks): `setSpatial`, `driveSpatial` (runs every frame, title screen included), `handNav`, setup (`startCalibration`, `calibrate`), tutorial (`startTutorial`, `tutStep`, `tutorialFrame`, `endTutorial`). `guide` is the setup or tutorial now running; `modeActive()` is false while it is, so the game is frozen underneath.
- `src/shell.html` / `src/dossier.css`: `#spatialView` (skeleton view; large and central during setup, flashes green when a step registers), `#spatialCard` (instructions, progress, Skip), `#handPointer`, `.hand-hover`.

### Setup and tutorial
- Turning tracking on with no saved profile starts **setup** (4 steps: frame your hand → pinch → fist and push → reach). "Skip setup" keeps the standard thresholds and does not ask again. Redo it from the title ("Recalibrate") or Pause ("Recalibrate hands").
- The first setup is followed by the **tutorial** (5 steps: look around, grab and drag, precision turn, two-hand inspect, fist push). Each step must register once. It runs in the office as a practice room: camera position is saved and restored, game state, save and clock are untouched, nothing is persisted except `localStorage['redacted-donor.hands.tutorial'] = 'done'`. "Skip tutorial" (or Esc) leaves it. Replay from the title ("Tutorial") or Pause ("Hand tutorial").
- Mid-game, setup and tutorial start only from free roam or the Pause menu, and end in the Pause menu.

### Security and privacy
`build.mjs` CSP allows `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net` (script + connect), `https://storage.googleapis.com` (connect) and `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`. Camera frames never leave the device; the privacy policy says so (`src/legal.js`).

### Not verified
- **Real hands on a real camera.** Everything above was tested with synthetic landmarks. Open questions for a real session: whether MediaPipe's world landmarks are steady enough for the depth fit; whether the look comfort zone (`LOOK_DEAD`) feels right; whether the grip thresholds (`gripOn` / `gripOff`) catch a natural grab without false grabs from a relaxed hand; whether the drawn hand's finger curl goes the right way (it assumes MediaPipe's world z is smaller toward the camera; flip the sign of the z term in `limb()` if fingers curl up instead of down); and the default thresholds used when setup is skipped.
- The Mac app's camera prompt. WebKit hides `navigator.mediaDevices` unless the app's `Info.plist` has `NSCameraUsageDescription`, so `native/Info.plist` now has one, and `Game.swift` grants the camera (never the microphone) to the bundled game page only (`requestMediaCapturePermissionFor`); macOS still asks the player once. Checked in a copy of the built app: the camera API is present and the hand model downloads and starts on the GPU. Not checked: the macOS prompt itself and a live camera picture.
